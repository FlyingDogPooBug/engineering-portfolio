> 01 / VEHICLE DYNAMICS · As of October 2026

As the suspension lead for a Formula Student car, I investigated how a change in front spring rate appeared in measured roll response.

| Item | Details |
|---|---|
| 7 runs | 2 baseline + 5 modified |
| 250 → 300 | Front spring rate · lb/in |
| ≈ 7.4% | Observed front-gradient reduction |

## The comparison

The front spring rate changed from 250 to 300 lb/in while the rear springs remained at 300 lb/in and the rear anti-roll-bar setup was retained. Suspension displacement and lateral acceleration were used to compare quasi-steady cornering roll gradients.

## My contribution

- Drove the car, adjusted suspension settings and analyzed raw data supplied by the instrumentation team.
- Compared calculations, analysis and measurements to document the setup decision. Sensor logging was the instrumentation team’s responsibility; I received assistance with programming.

## What we observed

Front roll gradient decreased from 1.182 to 1.095 deg/g, approximately 7.4%. The rear value increased from 1.031 to 1.045 deg/g, approximately 1.3%. Across filter settings of 2–5 Hz, the observed front reduction was approximately 7.1–7.4%.

## What the test does not establish

These were sequential runs, so tire temperature, pressure, road conditions and driver inputs were not fully isolated. I do not equate this result with a lap-time or grip improvement. A-B-A testing and further anti-roll-bar and damper checks remain future work.
